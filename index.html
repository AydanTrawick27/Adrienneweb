01 / THE PERSON BEHIND THE PURPOSE

Every calling
has a beginning.

Before I knew the kind of nurse I wanted to become, I knew what it felt like to need one.

I grew up in Washington, D.C., with an African American mother and a Caribbean father. Watching my mother work three, sometimes four jobs, and my father navigate barriers in his career as a software engineer taught me that hard work and opportunity do not always go hand in hand.

Those experiences shaped my awareness of inequality—and my desire to advocate for people whose voices and potential are too often overlooked.

The experience that changed my life ＋

During the COVID-19 pandemic, my mother was diagnosed with stage III breast cancer. She underwent two surgeries and was advised to complete a year of chemotherapy. In a time filled with uncertainty, her nurses became familiar faces, listening ears, and a source of reassurance for our family.

When I could not accompany her to treatment, they took time to communicate with me. Their courage and kindness showed me how deeply a healthcare professional can affect a family’s experience.

I began to understand that the stability I wanted was also emotional: knowing that my work had meaning and that my presence could make a difference.

THE LESSON I CARRY WITH ME

“Healthcare is not simply about treating a disease. It is about caring for the person experiencing it.”

RESEARCH SPOTLIGHT

Looking beyond the label.

With Dr. Davarpanah at Spelman College, I explored the role of zinc and titanium in products such as sunscreens, foundations, and blushes. The project encouraged me to consider ingredient transparency, consumer awareness, and how scientific information reaches the public.

Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are sunscreen active ingredients listed by the FDA.[1] Studying their uses reinforced a larger lesson: evaluate the evidence before drawing conclusions.

01Clinical science＋

Medical Microbiology introduced me to pathogens—microorganisms that can cause disease—as well as antibiotic resistance and infection control. Physiology helped me understand how the body’s systems function.

Future application: A scientific foundation for understanding illness and protecting patient safety during nursing training.

02Laboratory & data skills＋

Laboratory activities strengthened my ability to follow procedures, collect and interpret data, make careful observations, and draw evidence-based conclusions.

Future application: Accuracy, attention to detail, and respect for safe scientific practice.

03Technology & responsible AI＋

My research explored how artificial intelligence can help analyze scientific information, recognize patterns, and interpret complex datasets. I also learned that AI outputs require verification against credible scientific sources.

Future application: Using technology thoughtfully while keeping evidence and human judgment central.

04Case-based critical thinking＋

Case studies and classroom discussions taught me to analyze information, consider possible explanations, and support conclusions with scientific evidence.

Future application: Building the adaptability and reasoning I will continue to develop in nursing school.

Making knowledge

I want the science I learn to become information people can understand and use.

Health disparities are health differences linked to social, economic, and environmental disadvantage.[2] Understanding those barriers helps me connect individual patient care with a broader commitment to equity.
